Featured · Risk assessment · 15.30°N 61.39°W

How far can open data get you in a data-scarce island?

Small Island Developing States face some of the world's most acute flood risk, but rarely the data infrastructure to assess it in detail. This project asks a simple question: how far can free, globally available tools and open data get you toward building-level flood risk information in a data-scarce SIDS context? Hurricane Maria (2017), which caused damage equivalent to roughly 226% of Dominica's GDP in a single night, is the reference event that motivates the analysis and anchors the narrative.

Six FastFlood-modelled flood return period rasters (5–200 yr) are combined with Dominica's building footprint layer to classify individual buildings by flood frequency and depth threshold. Hazard and exposure are then combined into an island-wide risk raster following the UNDRR Risk = Hazard × Exposure framework, reclassified into four risk classes, and delivered as an interactive StoryMap: a methodology designed to be replicable in other data-scarce island contexts.
